# Cognitive Rewards: QA test cycle, 18 October 2026

**Verdict.** The core does what the product promises: no round can be solved alone, and the episode layer (vote, title, cold open, twists, a title for everyone) gives a game night a shape. The weak spots are uneven talking time per player, repetition in 4–5 player episodes, two small fairness bugs, and one key-ceremony fact that only the solver can know. None of these needs a redesign; all have small fixes, listed at the end.

## How this was tested

- Three full episodes on a local server with mock payment: **3, 4 and 5 players**, 6 + 8 + 10 = **24 rounds**, all phases from lobby vote to scorecard.
- The Game Master was played through a new local bridge instead of OpenRouter (no API credit used): the server wrote every request (safety filter, episode writer, judge) to a folder and the reply was written by the QA tester. The production prompts, validators and fallbacks ran unchanged; all three episodes passed validation.
- An automated team played every seat. For every round the screen of every seat was logged, and the information each player had to pass on was counted.
- Engine statistics from 3,000 simulated episodes per team size.
- Production Game Master quality is judged from the two episodes generated by the real model earlier today ("The Spare Is Inside", "Two Crews, One Stage").

**Not testable without people:** real enjoyment, real timings, and whether descriptions are as clear in speech as on paper. Times below are estimates (about 4 seconds per thing said, plus reading and answering). The playtest guide covers what to watch.

## Teamwork: does a round need what others know?

Every round's pieces were checked by the validator: drop any one player's piece and at least two answers remain. That held for all 24 rounds live and for every round in the automated tests. So in every round, the team needs every player. What differs is **how much** each player has to say and whether it is a conversation or a dictation.

| Round | Things each non-solver must pass on | Talk per round (team of 3 / 5) | Kind of talk | Est. time | Teamwork | Fun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common ground (cards) | 6 cards: object + colour | 18 / 30 | Real conversation: "anyone have a red key?" | 1.5–2 min (3p), 2.5–3.5 min (5p) | 5 | 4 |
| Calendar | 1–2 busy hours | 7 / 7 | Back-and-forth: "are you free at 14?" | about 1 min | 5 | 3 |
| Blueprint (grid) | 4–8 tiles | 16 / 16 | Dictation, then repeat-back | 1.5–2 min | 4 | 3 |
| Message thread | 1–2 messages with times | 6 / 7 | Read out, then sort together | about 1 min | 4 | 3 |
| Memory round | 0 pieces; recall the hour and the sticky note | 2 facts held by different people | "Who saw the note?" | 0.5–1 min | 4 | 4 |
| Line-up (faces) | **1 feature** | 2 / 4 | One sentence each | under 45 s | 4 | 3 |
| Code rules | **1 rule** | 5 (5p) | Read out, solver computes | 1–1.5 min | 3 | 2 |
| Snack count | **1 number** | 3 / 5 | One number each, solver adds | under 45 s | 3 | 2 |
| Desks (deduction) | 1–2 clues | not drawn in this cycle | Logic puzzle | 2–3 min | 4 | 2 after work |

Teamwork and Fun are the tester's 1–5 ratings. In the three episodes, about **half the rounds** were talk-heavy (cards, calendar, grid, thread) and half gave each non-solver a single sentence (line-up, snack, code, memory). In the light rounds the non-solvers are mostly listening.

### The secret key ceremony

Everyone must type the same three facts the team found earlier. In this cycle:

| Team | Facts asked | Who actually knew each fact |
|---|---|---|
| 3 players | free hour, snack total, **face you picked** | Hour and snack total were said out loud by the solver, so everyone heard them. The face number was a position on the solver's screen only. |
| 4 players | free hour, snack total, **face you picked** | Same, and there were **two** line-up rounds, so "the face you picked" is ambiguous. |
| 5 players | free hour, snack total, code | All three were built together and said out loud. |

The ceremony works as a team memory moment when the facts were spoken. The line-up fact breaks that: the witnesses never saw the numbers, so only one person can supply it, and with two line-ups nobody knows which one is meant.

## Engagement and fun

**What works**

- **Anticipation before the first round.** The vote, the episode title card and a cold open in the team's own words ("the beast warms up", "craft services ran out of cinnamon buns") make it feel like an event, not a test. Production samples stayed in-world and used the team's phrases naturally.
- **Pictures force real description.** Near-miss cards (a red key and a black key in the same hands) make "the key" useless and "the black key with the round bow" necessary. This is the best round in the game.
- **Twists change the feel of a round.** Flash ("look hard, then describe from memory") turns a dictation into a memory challenge; the server stops sending the piece after 30 seconds, so nobody can cheat.
- **Everyone leaves with a title**, and nobody sees anyone else's money.
- **The stuck flow** ("Anna needs your piece again", plus a tip for that round type) turns a dead end into a prompt to talk.

**What hurts**

- **Repetition.** 4- and 5-player episodes almost always repeat a mechanic (87% and 100% of simulated games). The 4-player test had two line-ups and two grids in eight rounds; the 5-player test had two line-ups and two card rounds.
- **Idle seats.** Line-up, snack and code rounds give non-solvers one sentence. With 5 players that is four people saying one thing each and then watching.
- **Back-to-back solver.** The same person solved two rounds in a row in the 5-player test (Eva, rounds 5 and 6). It happens in 35% / 24% / 18% of games with 3 / 4 / 5 players: solvers come from two shuffled passes, and the last of one pass can be the first of the next.
- **Rush on a 5-player card round** would be tight: about 30 things to compare in 150 seconds.
- **Flash on a 3-player grid** asks each source to memorise 8 tiles in 30 seconds. Doable, but more likely to frustrate than to delight.
- **Guessable answers.** Tap-to-answer rounds allow two tries out of six options: a solver who ignores the team still succeeds one time in three, and teammates still get help credit for it.
- **The final challenge rubric** gives up to 20 of 100 points for "callbacks" to the game, but the prompt never tells the team that. In the three tests the challenge scored 71, 52 and 49 mostly because the canned answer ignored the asked-for perk; real teams will do better, but they cannot know about the hidden criterion.

## Issues and recommended fixes (most important first)

| # | Issue | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Same solver twice in a row | 18–35% of games; seen live | When building the second pass, swap its first player if it equals the last of the first pass. |
| 2 | Line-up fact in the key ceremony only the solver can know; ambiguous with two line-ups | 2 of 3 test games | Drop the line-up from key facts, or ask for something everyone heard ("the colour of the hat"). Name the round in the label. |
| 3 | Repeated mechanics in 4–5 player episodes | 87% / 100% of games | One use per mechanic in 6- and 8-round episodes; in 10-round episodes at most one repeat, at least three rounds apart. |
| 4 | One-sentence rounds leave non-solvers idle | half of all rounds | Line-up: two features per witness in 3-player teams, or eight faces. Snack: mark "expired" with a picture, not a label, so each shelf needs describing. |
| 5 | Tap-to-answer rounds can be guessed | 1 in 3 blind success | Eight options instead of six, or one try plus a "confirm with the team" step. |
| 6 | Flash on a 3-player grid | 8 tiles in 30 s | Flash only on line-up and cards, or 45 s on grids. |
| 7 | Rush on a 5-player card round | about 30 comparisons in 150 s | No rush on card rounds with 5 players. |
| 8 | Hidden "callbacks" criterion in the final challenge | rubric vs prompt | Say "bonus for mentioning something from today's episode" in the prompt, or drop the criterion. |

Fixes 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 are small changes to the episode planner and the key-fact list, covered by the existing engine tests. Fixes 4 and 5 change round content and should be checked in the human playtest.

## Applied (18 October, same day)

All eight fixes are in, with tests in `app/lib/game/episode.test.ts` (24 tests pass) and a live check on a local game without AI calls.

| # | What changed | How it was checked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The second solver pass never starts with the player who ended the first. | 0 back-to-back solvers in 3,000 games per team size; test over 1,000 per size. |
| 2 | Key ceremony: free hour, one counted or rebuilt answer (snack, code or desks), and the sticky-note number read out in the memory round. Never a line-up position. | Test over every generated episode; live key ceremony showed hour, code and note. |
| 3 | No repeated mechanic in 6- and 8-round episodes. 10-round episodes: at most two repeats (not one: four talk mechanics cannot fill six talk slots with one repeat), different mechanics, at least three rounds apart. | Test over 1,000 plans per size; 0% repeats for 3 and 4 players. |
| 4 | Snack: items carry best-before days; only the solver's shelf shows today, so everyone reads out items and dates. Line-up: two details per witness in 3-player teams. | Live: a teammate's shelf showed two batches of the snack, best before the 12th and 23rd, without today's date; the witness screen showed two details. |
| 5 | Line-up has eight faces; in card rounds the solver holds the 32-card board instead of a hand. Blind success: 1 in 4 and 1 in 16 (was 1 in 3). | Test checks sizes; live board and 8-face line-up rendered, no overflow. |
| 6 | Flash only on line-up and card rounds. If flash fits nowhere, rush is used. | Test over 1,000 plans per size; live flash on a line-up hid the pieces after 30 s. |
| 7 | No rush on a 5-player card round. | Test over 1,000 plans. |
| 8 | Fixed bonus line under every final-challenge prompt, in all eight languages; the judge sees it too. | Test; the judge request in the live game carried the line. |

New strings (snack dates, card board, prompts, key labels, bonus line) are in all eight languages and in the regenerated translation sheet.
